## Across-run learning

- **R1→R2:** R1 was technical only, so no strategy learning could be extracted. The launch path was changed to a persistent interactive terminal; R2 prioritized clearer delivery and public results in response to known public feedback.
- **R2→R3:** affordability and visible costs were elevated as questions. R3 still ended with Wealth 0 and a debt narrative despite earlier resource/risk/health responses.
- **R3→R4:** the next run watched public costs, tangible delivery and risk signals. R4 completed at L5, but later had another public accumulated-risk episode; its score remained below S07-14.

The changes were adaptive and qualitative. No mechanism was held fixed across independent sessions; no public seed/replay control was found. This is not an A/B test or a repeatability estimate.

## Repeated public patterns

- R2 and R4 received public criticism that effort/activity was not translating into sufficiently visible wins or clear results.
- R3 and R4 both received explicit accumulated-risk feedback and later public “hazards exploded / work wasted” language after earlier risk-cleanup choices. The unresolved source and causal path are unknown.
- Promotion at M12 and M18 appeared in R2, R3 and R4, but their later outcomes diverged substantially. Promotion notices alone did not ensure survival or high score.
- R2 and R4 both completed 48 months but remained C; R4 reached L5 with Skill 97 while Output was 23. Final weighted score dimensions are missing, so this cannot be translated into score-weight explanations.

## Strategy hypotheses

### H1 — Visible outcomes may matter more than activity volume

- **Public evidence:** R2/R4 reviews repeated the “busy vs wins / clearer results” distinction; R4's M48 review gave an A bonus and L5 promotion after the player had communicated work and taken visible cross-team/mentoring responsibilities.
- **Possible mechanism:** evaluations may respond to legible, attributable delivery rather than raw effort or repeated project applications.
- **Counterexample / alternative:** R2 took multiple report/exposure actions and still ended L4/C; event paths, promotion criteria and score dimensions differ or are unknown. R4's promotion may reflect other unobserved public/hidden factors.
- **Expected benefit / stable-80 relevance:** could improve progression and performance evidence if verified, but score leverage is unknown.
- **Cheap-probe fit:** low for story/evaluation semantics; a scripted probe cannot substitute for Agent interpretation.
- **Need for real-Agent validation:** yes.
- **Compliance risk / complexity:** low if kept to general public feedback; low implementation complexity, but no Candidate change is currently justified.
- **Confidence:** LOW. **Falsifier:** repeated comparable public feedback plus stronger documented delivery fails to change review or progression in adequately repeated Agent runs.

### H2 — Affordability may be a separate survival constraint

- **Public evidence:** R3 reached L2 but was eliminated at M12 with Wealth 0 and explicit debt language; R2/R4 completed at Wealth 24/20.
- **Possible mechanism:** progression/Skill gains may not offset negative cash-flow pressure or costly commitments.
- **Counterexample / alternative:** one early-elimination run, stochastic event paths and incomplete public score dimensions; sources/timing of wealth changes are unknown.
- **Expected benefit / stable-80 relevance:** preventing financial collapse may protect completion, but no score effect is known.
- **Cheap-probe fit:** public trajectory diagnosis is feasible; environment response to alternative choices is not.
- **Need for real-Agent validation:** yes, for semantic cost/risk trade-offs.
- **Compliance risk / complexity:** low with visible money/costs only; modest if a compact unresolved-affordability check is later tested.
- **Confidence:** LOW. **Falsifier:** repeated runs maintain public financial stability while still failing, or explicit public debt signals prove non-predictive under comparable conditions.

### H3 — A risk action should not be treated as proof that the warning is resolved

- **Public evidence:** R3 and R4 received explicit accumulated-risk warnings; each included earlier risk-cleanup actions and later public “hazards exploded / work wasted” feedback.
- **Possible mechanism:** public risk may be multi-source, delayed or not directly observable; a cleanup action may not close the specific warning.
- **Counterexample / alternative:** we do not know whether the cleanup targeted the same cause, whether it had partial benefit, or whether later choices introduced new risks.
- **Expected benefit / stable-80 relevance:** could reduce tail loss if follow-up evidence can show whether a warning persisted; direct score leverage is unknown.
- **Cheap-probe fit:** weak unless the public interface exposes attributable before/after risk evidence; no hidden-state reconstruction is allowed.
- **Need for real-Agent validation:** yes, because linking narrative warnings to subsequent choices requires semantic interpretation.
- **Compliance risk / complexity:** low if limited to fresh participant-visible warnings/feedback; moderate if it becomes an ungrounded risk taxonomy.
- **Confidence:** LOW. **Falsifier:** public warnings resolve consistently without further evidence and later “risk explosion” is unrelated across repeated runs.
